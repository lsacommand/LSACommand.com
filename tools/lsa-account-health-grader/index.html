    How the score works

    Sixteen questions, 100 points, five areas. The weights are LSA Command’s own judgment of what usually moves a Local Services Ads account, not a formula from Google. Google doesn’t publish an account score. Where a question rests on a Google rule, the rule and its help page are listed below.

    AreaPointsWhat it checks

    • Answer rate scores 0 at 60% or less and full marks at 95% or more, in a straight line between. That range is our call, not Google’s.
    • Leads rated and leads per week also score in a straight line: full marks at 100% rated and at 10 leads a week.
    • Grades: A is 90 and up, B 80, C 70, D 60, F below 60.
    • Top 3 fixes are the questions losing the most points. When two lose the same amount, the one asked first wins, so the list leans toward calls and leads.

    The Google rules behind the questions

    • Ranking. Google ranks Local Services Ads on the bid, how likely the ad is to produce a lead (responsiveness, the search, relevance from services and business bio, and contact options such as message and booking leads) and profile quality (rating, number of reviews, average response time, high-quality images and verification checks). Google says missed calls may hurt responsiveness and recommends Maximize leads. Source: About ad rankings.
    • Missed calls. An unanswered call can be a charged lead when it comes in during the Business Profile hours (and ad schedule, if one is set) and the caller stays on for more than 20 seconds. Voicemail and automated greetings count as connected. Source: How leads work.
    • Message reply time. Google averages message response time over the last 90 days inside business hours (7 AM to 9 PM if none are set), weighted to recent leads, and says a consistently fast response time could improve ranking. Source: Manage leads and jobs.
    • Reviews. Star rating and number of reviews affect ranking, and customers are more likely to leave a review when the request comes from the business directly. Google gives each business a review link to share. Source: Reviews and ratings.
    • Credits and disputes. Google re-checks charged leads and credits some automatically, usually within 30 days. Leaving feedback can earn a bonus credit. Leads for job types or areas the business doesn’t serve are no longer credited. Credits aren’t available in health care, for tax specialists, or in EMEA. Source: About automated lead credits. A lead can only be disputed within 30 days. Source: Dispute a lead.
    • Lead volume. Google recommends budgeting for at least 10 leads a week. Source: How bidding works.

    What this grader can’t see

    • It only knows what you tell it. It doesn’t connect to the account, so a wrong answer gives a wrong grade.
    • Competitor comparisons are by eye, from a search in the client’s area. Results change by location and time of day.
    • It doesn’t check lead prices, invoices or individual charged calls. The missed-call cost calculator and budget calculator cover the money side.
    • Nothing you answer leaves your browser. “Copy link” puts the answers in the part of the link after the #, which isn’t sent to any server.

    Frequently asked questions

    Does Google give Local Services Ads accounts a health score?

    No. Google doesn’t publish an account score. It ranks ads on the bid, how likely the ad is to produce a lead and profile quality. This grader is LSA Command’s own checklist built around those factors.

    What affects Local Services Ads ranking?

    Google lists the bid, responsiveness, the customer’s search, relevance, contact options such as message and booking leads, and profile quality: rating, number of reviews, average response time, high-quality images and verification checks. See Google’s About ad rankings page.

    How often should an agency grade a client’s LSA account?

    Monthly works for most accounts, after the month’s leads are in and before the client report goes out. Re-grade after any change to hours, call routing or job types.
