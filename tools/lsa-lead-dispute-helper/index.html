      How the check works

      The helper runs the same checks, in the same order, that decide whether a credit is even possible. It stops at the first one that settles it.

      CheckIf…Result
      1. Charge statusNot charged, or already creditedNothing to dispute
      2. AccountHealth care, tax specialist, or Europe, the Middle East or AfricaCredits aren’t available
      3. In reviewThe lead shows “In review”Wait: Google is already deciding
      4. AgeMore than 30 days oldPast the window
      5. ReasonSpam, a sales pitch, a wrong number, a repeat within 15 days of the last contact, or a missed call outside the charged-call rulesGood case for a credit
      Something else that isn’t a real leadWorth flagging, no promise
      Job type or area the client doesn’t serve, a repeat after 15 days with no contact, or a real customer who didn’t bookGoogle says it doesn’t credit this

      “Good case” means the lead matches a rule Google publishes. “Worth flagging” means it may not be a real lead for the client, but Google’s pages don’t name that reason. Either way, Google decides.

      Weekly lead-credit checklist for agencies

      Once a week, for every client. A weekly habit keeps every lead well inside Google’s 30-day window.

      1. Filter last week’s leads to “Charged”. Leads that are Not charged need nothing.
      2. Check last week’s flagged leads. Note which ones changed to “Credited” and which are still “In review”.
      3. Listen to short and missed calls. A missed call is charged only in Business Profile hours (and the ad schedule) when the caller stays on more than 20 seconds. Anything else shouldn’t be charged.
      4. Look for repeat contacts. Once a lead is charged, follow-ups from the same number or email shouldn’t be charged again while there’s been contact in the last 15 days.
      5. Rate every lead, good and bad. Feedback is how Google learns what to send, and it can earn a bonus credit.
      6. Flag spam, sales pitches and wrong numbers with a short, specific reason. The helper above drafts it.
      7. Go oldest first. Anything three weeks old or more gets rated today.
      8. Fix settings, don’t dispute. Wrong job types and out-of-area leads aren’t credited, so turn those job types and areas off.
      9. Compare Business Profile hours with the hours someone answers. Wider profile hours turn into charged missed calls.
      10. Log credits for the client report. The original charge stays on the invoice; the credit goes to the account balance.
      11. Skip credit work where it doesn’t apply. For health care, tax and EMEA clients, put the time into targeting instead.

      Sources: How leads work, About automated lead credits, 30-day limit, lead quality in Google Ads.

      What Google’s pages don’t make clear

      • Whether a separate “dispute” button still exists in the LSA dashboard. For accounts in Google Ads, Google says manual lead disputes are no longer supported and points to “Rate this lead”. For the LSA dashboard, the newer pages describe automatic credits plus the feedback survey, and the “How to dispute charges” help link now opens the automated credits page, but an older Google page still says you can dispute a lead and get it credited in the US and Canada. We treat the feedback form as the way in. If an account still shows a dispute option, use the same text there.
      • Sales pitches and wrong numbers. Google’s Google Ads help page names solicitation and wrong numbers as invalid leads, but the Local Services Help pages only name spam and duplicates. We treat both as a good case either way.
      • Out-of-area leads after the move. The LSA credits page says “geo not serviced” leads are no longer credited, while the Google Ads page says out-of-area activity is adjusted automatically on the backend. Fixing the service area is still the reliable fix.
      • After 30 days. Google says disputes are accepted within 30 days and automatic credits usually land within 30 days. It doesn’t say whether feedback after that can still earn a credit.
      • Region wording. One Google page says no credits in EMEA, another says no automatic credits in EEA countries. Outside the US and Canada, check the account itself.
      • How much comes back. Google doesn’t publish whether credits are always the full lead charge.

      Frequently asked questions

      Can you still dispute a Google Local Services Ads lead?

      Mostly no. Google now reassesses charged leads, credits low-quality ones automatically, and asks advertisers to rate leads with the “Rate this lead” survey. For accounts in Google Ads, Google says manual lead disputes are no longer supported. An older Local Services page still describes disputes in the US and Canada, so check what the account shows, and act within 30 days.

      Which LSA leads does Google credit?

      Google says it filters spam and duplicate contacts and credits low-quality leads automatically, and its Google Ads help names solicitation and wrong numbers as invalid too. Follow-ups from the same number or email aren’t charged again while there’s been contact in the last 15 days. It no longer credits leads for job types or areas the business doesn’t serve. See About automated lead credits.

      Does a lead credit remove the charge from the invoice?

      No. Google says the original lead charge still appears on the invoice, and the credit is applied to the account balance, usually within 30 days.

      Does this tool send anything to Google?

      No. It runs in your browser and only drafts the text. You paste it into the lead’s feedback form yourself. Nothing you type is stored, and the details box is never put in the share link.
