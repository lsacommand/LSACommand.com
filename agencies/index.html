Why LSA is harder for agencies now

  • Missed calls can cost your clients money. Google can charge a missed call during business hours as a lead if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds. Voicemail doesn't protect the client, because Google treats a call that reaches voicemail during business hours as connected. Any client with a busy office is likely paying for some calls nobody picked up, and you're the one who has to explain the bill. See Google's page on how leads work.
  • LSA is moving into Performance Max. Google is moving Local Services Ads into Google Ads as Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals. The 14-day notice goes to the account administrator, which may be your client's inbox, not yours. Performance history doesn't come with it, weekly budgets turn into daily averages, and manual bids go away. Read Google's transition page.
  • Your reports can show a client at zero leads. A migrated account reports as Performance Max with local services enabled, not as a Local Services campaign. Any dashboard or script that filters for the old campaign type can quietly drop that client to zero while leads keep coming in. You find out when the client opens the report.
  • You can't dispute your way out of bad leads. Google has moved most lead credits to an automated system, and its guidance says it no longer credits "job type not serviced" or "geo not serviced" leads. What you feed Google matters more: rating every lead and keeping spam out of the count. Read Google's lead-credit guidance.
  • Leads slip through on accounts nobody is watching. Google's notification for a phone lead is an email with no recording, summary or transcript. In the LSA accounts we audit, 10 to 15% of paid leads routinely show no owner response at all, usually because the only notification was an email nobody saw. Multiply that by your client count.

How it works on every client account

LSA Command is built on the LSA API and handles the work that falls between "the lead came in" and "the client called back." The same four steps run on every account you connect.

  1. A lead comes in. A call or a message from the client's Local Services Ad.
  2. It gets answered. Calls ring the client's team first for 10 to 15 seconds. If nobody picks up, the call transfers and AI answers instantly, so calls are answered within 30 seconds. AI messaging responds instantly to message leads.
  3. Lead notes are written and added to Performance Max. An AI summary, the transcript and the recording, plus a Google LSA rating and an AI performance score. High-value leads identified. The notes are added to Performance Max for accounts on Google's Performance Max version of LSA.
  4. Notifications go out. A text and an email to the client, your agency, or both, with the notes and a link to the lead.

Yes. LSA Command has white-label options, so your agency stays out front with the client. You keep the relationship and set your own service offering. We provide the platform behind you.

If you are bringing a larger client book or want to resell LSA Command, book a walkthrough and we will walk you through the partner setup.

Does answering every call stop Google's missed-call charges?

Not exactly. Google's missed-call rule only applies to calls nobody answers. An answered call is judged like any other lead, so a real customer can still be a charged lead. The difference is that the customer gets answered and qualified instead of calling the next company on the list.

What should we do before a client's LSA account moves to Performance Max?

Find out who the account administrator is, because Google sends the 14-day migration notice to that person. Download the client's performance history, since Google says it does not transfer. Explain to the client that the weekly budget will be shown as a daily average. After the move, compare your report against the Leads page in Google Ads to confirm your reporting tool still counts every lead. Our Performance Max guide has more.

Who owns the client's Local Services Ads account?

That is between your agency and your client. LSA Command connects to an account with authorized access and does not change who owns it. Our advice is to put ownership in writing up front, including what happens to the account if the client leaves.

Can we start with one client?

Yes. Start with one client, see it working, and then roll it out to the rest of your book. Nothing goes live for a client until setup and billing have been confirmed with you.
