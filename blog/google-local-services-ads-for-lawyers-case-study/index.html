One of the few public data points on Google Local Services Ads for lawyers comes from Google itself. In 2022, Google published a case study about a law firm, Howe Law, and the agency that ran its Local Services Ads. The results panel reads: 300 leads in one month, 30 cases per month, and a 1,300% return on investment.

Google's write-up says the agency identified phone-routing gaps to increase responsiveness. In plain terms, that means finding the places where calls from the ad can go unanswered.

What Google's law firm case study reports

Every fact about the campaign in this section comes from Google's case study (2022). An agency ran the campaign for the firm, and Google wrote it up.

The challenge

Howe Law's approach to generating leads "required substantial spending per case." A limited monthly budget plus a high cost-per-acquisition (CPA) "was proving unsustainable."

Most firm owners know that position. The budget has a ceiling, each signed case costs too much, and spending more only makes the math worse.

The approach

The firm decided to make Local Services Ads its primary lead-generation tool "because it put their ads in front of customers searching for legal services in the local area."

"With a 60-day plan," the agency built a system that generated and tracked leads while also tracking return on investment (ROI). It also increased accountability "by identifying phone-routing gaps to increase responsiveness."

The study adds that LSA complemented other Google tools the firm used, like Google Business Profile.

The results

The results panel in Google's case study (2022) lists three numbers:

  • 300 leads in one month
  • 30 cases per month
  • 1,300% return on investment (the text calls it "a 1,300% average ROI")

The case study's headline describes new work booked at half the previous cost.

A caution before you put those numbers in a pitch deck. They are one firm's results, reported by Google for one campaign. The study does not say how much of the gain came from any single step, and it does not predict what another firm will get. Results vary.

Why answering every call matters for law firm Local Services Ads

The case study spells out the pricing model for Local Services Ads: businesses "only pay when a customer gets in touch directly from their ad."

So the ad's job ends when the phone rings. From that second on, the outcome depends on the firm. A lead that gets answered can become a consultation and then a case. A lead that rings out becomes nothing.

That is why the routing line in the study matters. A phone-routing gap is any point where a call from the ad goes somewhere it does not get answered. In a law firm, the usual ones look like this:

  • The ad points to a main line that nobody covers at lunch or after closing.
  • Calls forward to one attorney's cell, and that attorney is in court.
  • An intake line rolls to a voicemail box that is full or rarely checked.
  • Message leads sit in the Local Services Ads lead inbox until someone remembers to look.

Someone looking for a lawyer is often dealing with something urgent: an accident, an arrest, a custody deadline. If a call goes unanswered, they can call the next firm on the list.

Responsiveness is also something the firm can fix on its own. Budget, competition, and practice area are hard to change. Whether the phone gets answered is fully in the firm's control. For what Google itself says on the subject, see our post on the Google LSA phone response score.

A setup checklist for law firm Local Services Ads

The setup for law firm Local Services Ads has five parts. None is complicated. The last one decides whether the leads you pay for get answered. For the full walkthrough, see how to set up Google Local Services Ads.

1. Build the profile and pass verification

Lawyers go through Google's verification before ads can run, and firms that pass show the Google Verified badge. If verification stalls, see Local Services Ads background check and verification. Fill in the profile completely: practice areas, hours, and photos. Then start collecting reviews; see review generation. If the firm already has a Google Business Profile, keep the two consistent. In the case study, LSA complemented the firm's Google Business Profile.

2. Choose service areas and practice areas

Pick the areas you actually serve and the case types you actually want. See Local Services Ads categories. A family law firm that leaves every legal category switched on will get calls it cannot take.

3. Set a budget you can sustain

The firm in the case study started with a limited monthly budget. Start with a number the firm can hold for a few months, then raise it when the cost per signed case supports it. See Local Services Ads budget and bidding and what Google Local Services Ads cost.

4. Understand pay per lead

You are paying for contacts, not for clicks or impressions. That makes each lead easy to count, and it makes each unanswered lead easy to count too. See LSA missed call charges. If a lead looks invalid, here is how to dispute Google LSA leads.

5. Respond fast, every time

Before the ads go live, call the number yourself. Call at lunch, after closing, and on a Saturday. Send a message lead and time the reply. Every place the call or message stalls is a routing gap. Fix those first, because every dollar of budget runs through them.

What attorneys should measure in Local Services Ads

The agency in the case study built a system that tracked leads and ROI. A short scorecard is enough:

MeasureWhat it tells you
Leads per monthHow much demand the ads produce
Calls answered vs. missedWhether routing gaps are open
Time to reply on message leadsWhether messages are being worked
Cases signed per monthWhether leads turn into work
Cost per signed caseWhether the spend is sustainable
Return on investmentWhether to raise the budget

Google keeps the record of each lead in the lead inbox. Our guide to the Google LSA dashboard shows where to find it. Cases signed and fees collected live in the firm's own intake system, so someone has to connect the two. Without that connection, you cannot calculate a return.
