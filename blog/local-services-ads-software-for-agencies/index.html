Why Local Services Ads software for agencies matters more in 2026

An agency has one job across the client book: keep every account healthy. Two Google changes make that job harder to do by hand.

A missed call can be a charged lead. Google's current help page on how leads work says you pay for valid leads, and that a valid lead includes a missed phone call during your business hours where the customer stays on the line for more than 20 seconds. A call that goes to voicemail during business hours is treated as connected and charged on the same 20-second test. Our guide to LSA missed-call charges shows how to check a bill. For the full picture, see what happens when a Local Service Ads lead calls and nobody answers.

LSA is moving into Performance Max with pay-per-lead goals. Google's page on the move to Performance Max with pay-per-lead goals says existing LSA campaigns are being migrated automatically, in phases, to a specialized Performance Max campaign type with pay-per-lead goals. Ads still show on Search and Maps, and you still pay per lead. Three details matter for agencies:

  • The advance email goes to the account administrator 14 days before migration. That may be your client, not you.
  • Historical performance reports do not carry over, so download them first. Past leads do carry over to the Leads page.
  • In Google's words: "Manual lead disputes are no longer supported."

The agency migration playbook covers the steps. The takeaway here: speed and lead feedback now have to happen on every lead, on every account.

The checklist: six things to demand

1. Response speed: every call answered within 30 seconds

Ask the vendor for a number, not a promise. The standard is 100% of calls answered within 30 seconds, day or night, weekends included. The client's team should ring first for 10 to 15 seconds, so their people keep the first chance at every call. Then the voice AI answers. It should be able to transfer the call to the client's team and book appointments on the calendar. Message leads need the same speed: an instant AI reply. See how AI call answering and message lead response work, and what Google measures in the Google LSA phone response score.

2. Notes on every lead, plus instant notifications

Every lead should carry notes: an AI summary, the transcript and the recording. Ask where those notes live. The best answer is on the lead itself, written onto the lead in Google LSA, so the account's own record has the context. Then ask about notifications. They should go out instantly by text and email, under your agency's brand, to the client, the agency, or both. More on notes and notifications.

Ask what happens after the AI books a job. When the AI books an appointment on a Google LSA call, LSA Command's terminal bot fills out the Lead Summary directly inside Google's own LSA platform. It sets Lead Status to Booked, fills in Customer Name and Location, and writes the job and the appointment into Additional Details. No copy-pasting.

3. Priorities: High-value leads identified

A busy client cannot treat every lead the same way. Good software sorts them. Look for two things: High-value leads identified as they arrive, so the client knows which to call back first, and a weekly lead summary that rates each lead high or low value. That summary is also your answer when a client asks what the ads produced this week. For full client reporting, see LSA reporting software for agencies.

4. The busywork: survey, robocalls and lead feedback

Nobody on your team wants to rate every lead for every client. Ask whether the software does it: Google's 1 to 5 lead survey filled out on every lead, robocalls blocked before the client's phone rings with each block logged, and credit-eligible leads flagged. Ask how credits are counted, too. Google decides every credit, so no vendor should promise one, and a credit should only count once Google records it. More on lead disputes and credits.

Google's page on automated lead credits says charged leads are reassessed by its models over time and may be credited automatically. Google's page on lead quality for pay-per-lead campaigns says completing the "Rate this lead" survey on an invalid lead starts an automated evaluation, which can result in a reimbursement.

Set expectations with clients. Google's automated lead credits page also says it no longer supports credits for "job type not serviced" and "geo not serviced" leads, and that credits are not available for health care verticals, tax specialists, or advertisers in EMEA. If a client keeps getting unwanted leads, Google's advice is to revisit service areas, job types and categories.

5. White label and one agency workspace

Your clients hired your agency, so the software should carry your logo and your URL. You should also run the whole client book from one agency workspace. Ask whose name is on the text and email notifications, whose URL the client logs in at, and whether each client sees only their own leads. For what Google's own view shows per account, see the Google LSA dashboard. Related guides cover the neighboring jobs: multi-account LSA software (client separation, account mapping, lead routing), white-label Local Services Ads for agencies (packaging the service), and LSA CRM integration.

6. Pricing you can put in a proposal

You need a price you can quote without a spreadsheet. Look for a flat per-client price with everything included and no setup fee. Ask about per-minute voice fees, setup fees, minimums and contract terms. Then you can set your own client price and know your margin on day one. Google's lead charges are separate. Google bills those to the advertiser. LSA Command pricing explained breaks down the math, and the setup guide shows the one-call setup.
