What the Local Services Ads lead inbox is

The lead inbox is where every Google LSA lead lands. Google's help page on managing your leads says that from your lead inbox you can review booked jobs, listen to phone lead recordings (US only), respond to leads, and review phone calls generated by your ad.

Open any lead and you get a panel called the Lead Summary. It holds the fields that describe the lead: Lead Status, Customer Name, Location, Additional Details and a few more.

Here is the catch. A phone call does not mark itself as booked. The Booked status, the job and the appointment only get into the Lead Summary if somebody goes in and adds them. On a busy account, that step is easy to skip.

So the Google LSA lead inbox fills up with calls that all look alike. A booked job and a wrong number sit side by side with the same empty fields.

The blue Booked status in the lead list

The Lead Summary is the detail view. The payoff shows up one level higher, in the lead list.

Once Lead Status is set to Booked, the lead carries a blue Booked status in the Lead status column. Other rows show Completed or Active. You can scan the column and count booked jobs in seconds.

That one column answers the question every client asks: "Are these ads turning into work?" We built this first for our own agency's Google LSA accounts.

How to mark a lead as booked yourself in Google's lead inbox

You can do all of this by hand. Google's help page on Local Services Ads reports gives the steps:

  1. Open the Local Services Ads inbox.
  2. Choose a lead.
  3. Select Mark Booked.
  4. Select the date, or indicate that the job is completed.
  5. Add details for tracking.
  6. Save.

The same page explains why it is worth doing. Google says Local Services ad reports help you "Measure if those leads convert to bookings." When you mark a lead as booked, you can add details like job type and customer name to track your leads, and you can use the filters on the Reports page to find those leads later.

Two notes from Google: these details are only visible to you, and marking leads as booked is currently available only in the U.S. and Canada.

Six steps is not hard. Six steps on every booked call, on every client account, every week, is where it falls apart. Somebody has to listen to the call, find the lead, and type the details. Most teams mean to and do not.

Why it matters for agencies

Google's own lead records stay accurate. The lead inbox is where Google keeps the record of every lead on a Local Services Ads account. When booked calls are marked there, the record matches what happened on the phone.

Booked jobs are visible at a glance. You and your client can open Google's LSA platform and see the blue Booked statuses. No second login, no export, no spreadsheet.

Client conversations get shorter. When a client says the leads are no good, you can open their own Google account and point to the booked jobs, each with the job and the appointment written on it.

One thing we do not claim: that marking a lead as booked changes how Google ranks or bills an account. Google describes it as a way to track leads and measure bookings, and that is how we use it.
