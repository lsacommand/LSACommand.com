How Google LSA lead disputes work now

Google's help page on lead quality for campaigns with pay-per-lead goals states the new process directly: "While manual lead disputes are no longer supported, invalid leads are automatically detected and credited by backend systems. If you receive an invalid lead, select the lead on the Leads page and complete the Rate this lead survey. Submitting this feedback initiates an automated evaluation, which can result in a reimbursement reflected under your Charge Status column."

The older Local Services Ads dashboard runs on automated credits too. Google's automated lead credits page says leads are assessed at first contact, and invalid or low-quality leads are not charged. Charged leads are then reassessed by Google's models over time and may be credited automatically.

Google is also moving existing LSA campaigns, in phases, into a Performance Max campaign type with pay-per-lead goals. Its transition page repeats the point: "Manual lead disputes are no longer supported."

So "disputing" a lead today means two things: giving Google feedback, and watching the lead's Charge Status. Google's how leads work page lists four statuses (Charged, Not charged, In review, Credited) and says lead quality is assessed over a 30-day period.

How to dispute Google LSA leads, step by step

  1. Find the lead. In a Local Services Ads account, open the menu and go to Leads. If the account has moved into Google Ads, go to Goals, then Conversions, then Leads. Select the lead.
  2. Check its Charge Status. Google uses four statuses: Charged, Not charged, In review and Credited. Not charged or Credited means there is nothing to recover. In review means Google is still evaluating. Charged is the one to work on.
  3. Confirm what actually happened. Listen to the recording or read the message thread. Be careful with missed calls: Google's current help page says a missed call during business hours is a valid lead if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds. A missed call is not automatically a disputable lead. See how to check a missed-call charge.
  4. Complete the Rate this lead survey. With the lead selected, choose Rate this lead and answer honestly. Google says submitting this feedback initiates an automated evaluation, which can result in a reimbursement shown in the Charge Status column. Rate the good leads too: Google says the survey helps it send better-suited leads.
  5. Wait for Google's automated evaluation, and keep a record. Google says that in most cases credits are applied within 30 days, and the original charge still appears on the invoice. Log the lead ID, your rating and the date you submitted it, then check Charge Status again after about 30 days. Google decides the outcome.
  6. Fix the setting that let the lead in. If the lead was the wrong job or the wrong area, no credit is coming. Change the setting so that lead type stops arriving. The next section covers how.

Fix the settings that create bad leads

Because Google no longer credits "job type not serviced" and "geo not serviced" leads, prevention is the only fix for those. Google's own advice, if you are receiving unwanted leads, is to revisit your service areas, job types and categories.

  • Job types: switch off every service the business does not want to be called about.
  • Service area: trim it to where the crew will actually drive. See the guide to LSA service area targeting.
  • Categories: make sure the profile is listed only under the work the business does.
  • Business hours: missed calls during business hours can be charged, so list the hours when calls will be answered. Answer speed matters here too: see the Google LSA phone response score.

Frequently asked questions

Can I still dispute a Google LSA lead manually?
No. Google's help pages say manual lead disputes are no longer supported. Instead, select the lead on the Leads page and complete the Rate this lead survey. Google says that feedback initiates an automated evaluation, which can result in a reimbursement shown in the Charge Status column. Google decides the outcome.
How long do LSA credits take?
Google says that in most cases credits are applied within 30 days. The original charge still appears on the invoice, so check the lead's Charge Status for Credited instead of waiting for the charge to disappear.
Why didn't I get a credit for an out-of-area lead?
Google no longer supports credits for geo not serviced or job type not serviced leads. A lead from outside your preferred area can still be billable. The fix is in your settings: Google's guidance is to revisit your service areas, job types and categories.
Am I charged for missed calls?
You can be. Google's current help page says a missed phone call during your business hours is a valid lead when the customer stays on the line for more than 20 seconds. A call that goes to voicemail or an automated greeting during business hours is treated as connected and is charged on the same 20-second rule. A missed call is not automatically a lead Google will credit.
