What changed: you can now download Local Services Ads historical reports

When Google first described the move into Google Ads, its help article said Local Services Ads performance reports would not transition, that a download would come "soon" on the redirection page, and that anyone who needed reports right away should take screenshots before migrating. Search Engine Roundtable quoted that earlier wording from an archived copy dated October 26, 2026.

On November 17, 2026, Search Engine Roundtable reported that the download is now available. Google's Google Ads Help article on the transition to Performance Max with pay-per-lead goals now says, in its "keep in mind" box: "You can download your historical Local Services Ads reports on the redirection page."

One honest caveat: when we checked the article, its FAQ section still used the older future-tense wording ("A dedicated download page will be available"). The top of the page and the news coverage say it is live. If your redirection page does not show a download yet, keep screenshotting and check again.

What the Google Local Services Ads reports download includes

Google's own description is short. The download "includes your lead totals (total lead spend and number of charged leads, split by phone, message, and booking)".

What Google has not said, in anything we could verify: the file format, the date range inside the file, whether it is per account or per location, and whether a manager account can pull files for many linked accounts at once. Treat all of those as "check on your own redirection page". For reference, Google's View reports on Local Services Ads page says on-dashboard report data is available from October 1, 2017 onward, but Google does not say the download covers that same range.

What the download leaves out of your Google Local Services Ads reports

Google is direct about it: the download "does not include impressions, clicks, or other report data." The FAQ on the same help article adds that previous campaign-level performance metrics, "such as past impressions, clicks, weekly spend, and ad-level performance reports", will not migrate.

So if a client report, a monthly review or a pricing conversation leans on any of those numbers, capture them before the account moves:

  • Screenshot the views you use. Set the date range you care about, then capture each view in full. Name every image with the client and the date range.
  • Copy key numbers into a sheet. A screenshot is proof. A sheet row is something you can compare next quarter.
  • Note lead credits separately. Google's reports page lists lead credits received, and Google's description of the download does not mention them. If credits matter to the client's history, record them yourself.

How to download Local Services Ads historical reports, step by step

These steps follow Google's help text. Button names on the redirection page can vary, and Google has not published a screenshot-level walkthrough, so confirm each step on your own screen.

  1. Sign in with the right Google account. Use the account that has access to the client's Local Services Ads account. If you manage many clients, our guide to managing client LSA from a manager account covers access.
  2. Open the redirection page. Use the redirection page Google links from its help article (the link sits in the transition help article, on the line about downloading historical reports).
  3. Find the historical reports download. If it is not there yet, screenshot the Reports page (Local Services Ads inbox, then the menu, then Reports, per Google's reports help page) and check again later.
  4. Download and rename the file. Use the client name and the download date, for example roofing-client-1_lsa-history_2026-12-14.
  5. Open it and check it. Confirm you see total lead spend and charged leads by phone, message and booking. Write down the date range and format you actually got.
  6. Capture what is missing. Screenshot impressions, clicks and weekly spend views, and save them in the same client folder.

If you run many accounts and have developer help, Google's reports page also notes that the Local Services API lets manager (MCC) accounts access reporting data for all managed accounts. Check that it still covers your accounts before you rely on it. Our post on the Local Services Ads API explains what it can and cannot do.

A backup routine to download Local Services Ads historical reports for every client

For one account, the steps above take minutes. For 20 or 40 clients, the risk is not difficulty. It is that one account migrates before anyone remembers to back it up. A small routine fixes that.

Free checklist: back up every client's LSA history

  1. One folder per client, named the same way in every tool you use.
  2. File name template: client-name_lsa-history_YYYY-MM-DD. Screenshots: client-name_lsa-screens_YYYY-MM-DD_view-name.
  3. A tracker row per client: migration date (or "check account"), report downloaded, file name, screenshots saved, checked by.
  4. Open every file once. Note the date range and format you actually received.
  5. Recheck weekly any client whose migration date is not known yet.
  6. Once a client has moved, confirm their lead history shows on the Leads page in Google Ads.

Using the file: cost per charged lead from Google Local Services Ads reports

Lead totals are still useful. Two quick numbers per client give you a baseline to compare against once the account runs inside Google Ads:

  • Average cost per charged lead: total lead spend divided by total charged leads.
  • Lead mix: the share of charged leads that were phone, message and booking.

Only split spend by lead type if your own file shows spend that way. Google's description is clear that charged leads are split by type; read your file before you assume the same for spend. Also note when each charge happened relative to billing changes: Google began charging for some missed calls in 2026 (missed calls during business hours where the caller stays on the line more than 20 seconds, per Google's How leads work help page), which we cover in how to check your LSA bill for missed call charges.

Frequently asked questions

Can I download my historical Local Services Ads reports?

Yes. Google's Google Ads Help article on the move to Performance Max with pay-per-lead goals says you can download your historical Local Services Ads reports on the redirection page. Search Engine Roundtable reported the change on November 17, 2026. The file covers lead totals only, so plan to capture anything else another way.

What is in the Local Services Ads historical report download?

Google says the download includes your lead totals: total lead spend and the number of charged leads, split by phone, message and booking. Google has not said in the text we checked what file format it uses or what date range it covers, so open your own file and confirm both.

Does the download include impressions or clicks?

No. Google says the download does not include impressions, clicks, or other report data. If you rely on any of those views, take screenshots of them before the account migrates, because Google also says previous performance reports will not carry over to Google Ads.

Do I lose my lead history when my LSA account moves to Google Ads?

No. Google says past customer lead history, including contact details, message history and older call recordings, transfers to the Leads page in Google Ads, under the Goals menu (some accounts show it under Goals, then Conversions). What does not transfer is campaign-level performance data such as past impressions, clicks, weekly spend and ad-level reports.

Where is the Local Services Ads redirection page?

It is the page Google shows Local Services Ads accounts that are moving to Google Ads. Google's help article on the transition links to it directly (ads.google.com/localservices/gaferedirection). The exact layout and button names can differ by account, so sign in with the Google account that has access and check what your page shows.

Should agencies download Google Local Services Ads reports for every client?

Yes. Download the file for each client before that client's migration, save it with the client name and download date in the file name, add screenshots of anything the file skips, and log it in one tracker so nobody has to guess which accounts are done.
