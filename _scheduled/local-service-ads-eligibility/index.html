Local service ads eligibility comes down to five Google LSA requirements: the right country, a category Google lists for that country and state, a public and verified Google Business Profile, the screening Google sets for that category, and the lead types Google offers there. You can check all five in about a minute, then ask Google to confirm.

The 1-minute local service ads eligibility self-check

Use this before a sales call or before you quote a new client. Every row comes from a public Google page, so you need no login. Open Google's Getting started page and its screening and verification requirements page, then answer five questions.

This is the quick yes or no screen. Our Local Services Ads categories list holds the full category list and the longer pre-sale category check. To confirm category and area, use Google's own public "Check eligibility" step on the Getting started page: "To check if Local Services Ads are available in your area, check your business eligibility."

One more item sits behind all five: the Google Ads account itself. Google's How leads work page says, "Billing for Local Services Ads is managed by Google Ads. Any suspension in your Google Ads account will also suspend your Local Services Ads."

Why the Business Profile row is strict. Search Engine Roundtable (October 10, 2024) published Google's notice that by November 21, 2024 a Local Services ad "will need a matching Google Business Profile." On March 7, 2025 it reported Google's follow-up email: "From now, you must have an affiliated public Google Business Profile to run Local Services Ads."

Google LSA requirements change by category and state

Google's U.S. requirements page says background check, business registration and insurance requirements "are only required for select users," then lists exceptions category by category.

  • Urgent categories get extra screening. Google calls plumbing, HVAC, electrician, garage door and locksmith "urgent" categories and says providers "undergo an additional level of screening, which includes service professional background checks."
  • Garage door and locksmith go further. Both also go through "Google's Advanced Verification screening process," and Google says pre-badge ads are not available for garage door services, health care verticals or locksmiths.
  • Seven categories are state-limited. Architect, drain expert, home insulation, insurance agency, interior designer, piercing studio and tattoo studio are marked California and Florida only.
  • Allow time. Google says screening and verification takes 3 to 4 weeks on average after documents are submitted, and recommends starting as soon as the account exists.

Google's own local service ads eligibility check

A self-check is a good guess. For a connected account, Google now gives a firmer answer. Local Services Ads are moving into Google Ads (Google's transition page describes the move to Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals), and the Google Ads API gained an eligibility method on the way.

Google's Google Ads API release notes for v26.1 (October 28, 2026) list a new method, CheckLocalServicesEligibility, "to check Local Services eligibility for a customer and Google Business Profile listing ID." Search Engine Roundtable (October 28, 2026) summed it up: it "checks whether an account and Google Business Profile listing qualify for Local Services" and "can return eligible lead types, service categories, job types, and business callouts." The release notes add reasons that explain eligibility issues, "such as licensing issues."

LSA Command runs that method for you:

  • It needs a connected account. The check runs for a business whose Google Ads account is connected to LSA Command. For a prospect with no connection, use the self-check above and Google's public "Check eligibility" step.
  • It finds the listing. LSA Command looks for the Business Profile listing in an existing Local Services campaign or in the account's Business Profile location asset. If it finds more than one, you pick the listing.
  • It is read-only on Google. Nothing in the Google Ads account changes.
  • It returns one of six statuses: eligible, not eligible, no listing found, choose a listing, invalid listing, or check failed.
  • When eligible, it shows the detail. Each eligible lead type appears with its eligible categories: category name, job types and callouts.
  • Google's reasons appear in Google's own words, both the short and the long text, so a licensing issue reads the way Google wrote it.
  • It repeats. The check runs automatically once a day for connected accounts, and on demand.

Frequently asked questions about local service ads eligibility

What are the Google LSA requirements in short?

Google's public pages point to five checks: the business is in a country where Local Services Ads run, its category is on Google's list for that country (and state, for some categories), it has a public and verified Google Business Profile, it can pass the screening Google lists for its category (license, insurance and background checks vary), and the lead types it wants are offered there.

How do I check local service ads eligibility for a client?

Start with the 1-minute self-check in this post, then confirm with Google. Since Google Ads API v26.1 (October 28, 2026), Google offers a method named CheckLocalServicesEligibility that returns whether an account and its Business Profile listing are eligible, plus the eligible lead types and categories.

Which Local Services Ads categories are limited to certain states?

On Google's U.S. Getting started page, checked November 24, 2026, seven categories are marked California and Florida only. They are named in the category section of this post.

Does a business need a Google Business Profile for Local Services Ads?

Yes. Google's screening and verification requirements page says Local Services advertisers must have a public and verified Google Business Profile. Search Engine Roundtable reported the same from a Google email on March 7, 2025.

Can every eligible business get call, message and booking leads?

No. Google says booking leads are available only in the United States and Canada, and are not available for health care verticals. Google's eligibility method returns the eligible lead types for each account.
