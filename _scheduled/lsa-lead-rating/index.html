What a Google Local Services Ads lead rating is, and how Google collects it

Google's help page on automated Local Services Ads lead credits gives the steps: open Leads, select "Rate this lead" in the top right corner, and submit the survey. Google calls it the Lead Feedback Survey. The same page says feedback can also be submitted with the Google Ads API.

Google's published API definition spells out what the survey holds. The survey answer is one of five steps: very satisfied, satisfied, neutral, dissatisfied or very dissatisfied. A satisfied or dissatisfied answer carries a reason, and Google's lead service definition says a free form comment is required when the reason is "Other".

Why your Google Local Services Ads lead rating matters to Google

Google tells advertisers two things about this feedback.

  • It shapes the leads you get. Google's How leads work page says: "If you believe the lead you received is poor quality, you can let us know in the Feedback Survey for that lead. We'll do our best to send you more of the leads that you want and fewer of those you don't want."
  • It can lead to a bonus credit, at Google's discretion. The same page adds: "We may also occasionally credit leads you report as poor quality through this survey." When a rating is sent through the API, Google answers with a bonus credit decision: issued, not issued because the bonus credit cap was reached, or not issued because the lead is not eligible. Google's definition says an issued bonus credit "will be available for use in about two months."

Google has asked for this since it automated lead credits. Search Engine Roundtable (June 25, 2024) quoted Google's Ads Liaison: "we are also opening an in-product Lead Feedback survey on every lead." A later report (February 11, 2025) quoted the same source: "We also encourage advertisers and agencies to provide feedback on all leads in their accounts".

Three limits keep expectations honest. Google's credits page says it "no longer supports credits for 'job type not serviced' and 'geo not serviced' leads," and that lead credits are not available for health care verticals, tax specialists or advertisers in EMEA. Google's transition page says "Manual lead disputes are no longer supported." And Google alone decides a credit. We cover that side in how to dispute Google LSA leads and LSA lead disputes for agencies, and the causes of poor leads in why you are getting bad Google LSA leads.

Since Google says it uses this feedback to decide which leads to send more of, a wrong rating tells Google the wrong thing about what your client wants. That is the case for keeping a person in control of what gets sent.

How AI lead scoring works for LSA leads in LSA Command

"AI lead scoring" usually means software judging how good a lead is. AI lead scoring for LSA has a second job: the score has to fit the survey Google already uses. In LSA Command, AI rates each new LSA lead from its call or its messages. The result is Google's 5-step lead rating (very satisfied through very dissatisfied) with a reason and an optional comment.

Everything lives on one page, Lead Ratings, described in the product as: "Control how AI ratings are sent to Google for your LSA leads. Approve held ratings here and see who approved each one." You select a business, then choose how much the AI may send on its own.

Review first and strictness: who sends each Google Local Services Ads lead rating

One checkbox sets the mode: "Send AI ratings to Google automatically."

  • Turned off, that is review first. Every AI rating waits until someone approves it. Nothing reaches Google without a decision from your team.
  • Turned on, strictness decides. You pick one of three levels, and the level decides which ratings go straight to Google and which are held.
SettingWhat happens to a new AI rating
Review first (automatic sending off)Every AI rating waits until someone approves it.
StrictSend only satisfied ratings automatically.
BalancedHold dissatisfied ratings for review.
RelaxedSend every AI rating.

Settings apply to new AI ratings only. Our suggestion: start a new client on review first, then loosen as the ratings earn trust.

Approve, edit or reject: AI lead scoring with the transcript beside it

Held ratings collect in the "To review" tab. Each one shows the customer, the lead type, the job type and the date, plus a pill that says why it is waiting: "Review first" or "Held by strictness." Under that sit the AI's rating, its reason and its comment.

"Show transcript" opens the call or message thread right beside the rating, so the reviewer checks the AI's reasoning against what was really said. Then there are three choices:

  • Approve & send to Google. The rating goes to Google as written.
  • Edit. Change the rating, the reason or the comment before it is sent. A comment is required when the reason is Other, which matches Google's own rule.
  • Reject. Rejected ratings are never sent.

The approval log: an audit trail for every Google Local Services Ads lead rating

The second tab, "Approval log," records what happened to each rating in five columns: Lead, Rating sent (or "Not sent"), Decision, By, and When. "By" shows the person who decided, or "Automatic (AI)" when the rating went out under your automatic settings.

For an agency, that answers the questions a client eventually asks. Who told Google this lead was bad? When? Was it the AI or someone on the account? Select the business and walk the owner through the decisions line by line.

Frequently asked questions about Google Local Services Ads lead rating and AI lead scoring

What is a Google Local Services Ads lead rating?

A Google Local Services Ads lead rating is the feedback you give Google on one lead. In the leads list you select Rate this lead and fill in Google's Lead Feedback Survey. Through the Google Ads API the same survey has five answers, from very satisfied to very dissatisfied, plus a reason and an optional comment.

Does a Google Local Services Ads lead rating get a credit from Google?

Not on its own. Google says it uses the feedback to send more of the leads you want and fewer of those you do not, and that it may also occasionally credit leads reported as poor quality. Google decides every credit. LSA Command does not promise credits.

Is AI lead scoring the same thing as Google's lead rating?

Not quite. AI lead scoring usually means software judging how good a lead is. In LSA Command the AI reads the call or message thread and scores each new LSA lead on Google's own 5-step rating, with a reason. That is the rating Google can receive. Separately, High-value leads identified tells your team which leads to work first.

Can I stop AI ratings from going to Google until someone approves them?

Yes. On the Lead Ratings page, turn off Send AI ratings to Google automatically. That is review first mode: every AI rating waits until someone on your team approves it.

What do Relaxed, Balanced and Strict mean?

They are the strictness levels that apply when automatic sending is on. Relaxed sends every AI rating. Balanced holds dissatisfied ratings for review. Strict sends only satisfied ratings automatically. Settings apply to new AI ratings only.

What happens when I reject an AI rating?

A rejected rating is never sent to Google. The approval log records the lead, shows Not sent, and lists the decision, who made it and when.
