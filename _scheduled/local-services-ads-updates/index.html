The biggest local services ads news: LSA moves into Google Ads

The move is announced, in three phases

July 21, 2026 · Sources: Search Engine Roundtable, Google Ads Help

What changed: Existing LSA campaigns are being migrated into a specialized Performance Max campaign type with pay-per-lead goals. Google's phases: August 2026 for select US home and storefront services (plumbing, HVAC, electrical, roofing and others), late 2026 for service-area businesses without storefronts and accounts with custom bidding or booking setups, and 2027 for non-US accounts and remaining categories. Google says you still pay only for valid leads, and ads still show only on Search and Maps.

What to do: List every client, its category and its likely phase. Make sure the account administrator's inbox is watched, because Google sends the migration email 14 days ahead.

Go deeper: the agency migration playbook.

What changes on migration day

Per Google's help page, as of November 20, 2026 · Source: Google Ads Help

What changed: Weekly budgets become daily averages (weekly divided by 7), with monthly spend capped at the daily average times 30.4. Manual bidding and industry-level Target CPA are deprecated, so per-category targets become one campaign-level target. BBB callouts are gone. The lead inbox becomes the Leads page in Google Ads, and old dashboard access redirects there. Lead history carries over; performance reports do not. Verified status and the Google Verified badge carry over. A new toggle controls email notifications for call and message leads, and every user is opted in by default. Google also notes that manual lead disputes are no longer supported.

What to do: Write down each client's bids, budgets and per-category targets before the move. If you need separate targets for two categories, Google suggests separate campaigns. After the move, check who receives lead notifications.

Local services ads updates to how leads are charged

Missed calls can be charged

Reported August 25, 2026, effective October 1, 2026 · Sources: Search Engine Roundtable, Google, How leads work

What changed: Google's email to advertisers said: "On October 01, 2026, we are updating how and when you are charged for call leads." Google's help page now says a call you miss during business hours is a valid lead if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds. If a phone menu needs a key press, the timer starts at the first key press. A call that reaches voicemail or a greeting during business hours is treated as connected and can be charged, message or not. Unanswered calls outside business hours or the ad schedule are not charged.

What to do: Check every client's Business Profile hours against the ad schedule. Look for hold queues, voicemail and phone menus on the call path. Then estimate the cost with our free LSA missed call calculator.

How leads work page expanded: charge statuses and the 15-day rule

October 8, 2026 · Sources: Search Engine Roundtable, Google, How leads work

What changed: Each lead now shows a Charge Status: Charged, Not charged, In review or Credited. Google says it assesses lead quality over a 30-day period. Once a lead is charged, follow-up calls or messages with that customer are not charged again if there is interaction within 15 days from the same phone number or email.

What to do: Review Charge Status weekly per client, and use the survey on each lead to give Google feedback. Do not promise clients credits; Google decides those.

Local services ads news on verification and badges

D-U-N-S numbers for some verticals, and a 30-day grace period

July 31, 2026 · Sources: Search Engine Roundtable, Google Ads Help

What changed: Google began telling some advertisers it would require a D-U-N-S number for select verticals and users in the US, with alternative documents if one cannot be obtained. Google's verification page for pay-per-lead campaigns says most businesses can start serving and have a 30-day grace period to finish checks. Miss it and the campaign turns Not Eligible. Locksmiths and garage door services get no grace period.

What to do: Check every client's verification tab now, and get registration documents ready before they are asked for.

Go deeper: the 30-day verification deadline.

Policies renamed, pre-badge ads added, badge exclusions widened

August 5 and August 21, 2026 · Sources: SER, requirements, SER, pre-badge ads

What changed: "Local Services platform policies" became "Local Services Ads requirements", with examples of violations and tiered consequences. Pre-badge ads let businesses get leads after preliminary checks while onboarding finishes (not for garage door, health care or locksmiths). Many more categories became eligible, and the Google Verified badge is now unavailable for auto, beauty and dining. That badge replaced Google Guaranteed, Google Screened and License Verified starting October 2025, per Search Engine Journal (August 21, 2025).

What to do: Read the new requirements page with each client once. Check whether new categories fit clients you turned away before.

Go deeper: the Google Verified badge explained.

Local services ads updates to the ad and lead types

Home listings for real estate agents

June 12, 2026 · Source: Search Engine Roundtable

What changed: Google rolled out Local Services Ads for home listings across all 50 US states, powered by HouseCanary data. Buyers can call, message or book an agent from the listing. Existing LSA agents appear automatically.

Go deeper: Google LSA for real estate agents.

Booking partners expand, and bookings become a goal

August 26, 2026 · Sources: Search Engine Roundtable, Google Ads Help

What changed: Google's Ads Liaison said booking now works with "more than 500 Reserve with Google booking partners, up from just 20." An active partner booking link on the Business Profile is enabled for the ad automatically, and direct bookings count as paid leads. In pay-per-lead campaigns, bookings are an optional goal, available starting October 2026.

What to do: Check which clients have a partner booking link live, and decide on purpose whether bookings should be on.

Featured professionals removed, headshots optional

October 19, 2026 · Source: Search Engine Roundtable

What changed: An in-account notice said: "Starting in October, featured professionals will no longer show on your ads. Headshot is optional and won't affect ad serving." This mostly affects law firms.

Go deeper: Google LSA for lawyers.

Local services ads news for reporting: historical downloads

Historical reports can be downloaded

November 17, 2026 · Sources: Search Engine Roundtable, Google Ads Help

What changed: Google's help page says you can download historical reports on the redirection page. The file includes lead totals (total lead spend and charged leads, split by phone, message and booking) and "does not include impressions, clicks, or other report data."

What to do: Download the file for every client and save it with the date in the name. Screenshot impressions and clicks before the dashboard goes away.

What agencies should do now: a local services ads updates checklist

Here is the checklist we use, free to copy. Run it once per client.

  • Download the historical report from the redirection page, and save it with the date in the file name.
  • Screenshot impressions and clicks, since the download does not include them.
  • Write down bids, weekly budget and any per-category Target CPA.
  • Confirm who the account administrator is, so the 14-day migration email is seen.
  • Check Business Profile name, address, hours and primary category. Big changes trigger a review that can pause the campaign.
  • Check the verification tab and the 30-day grace period.
  • After migration, set lead notifications for the right users and pick at least 6 callouts.
  • Test the call path during business hours: no long hold, no full voicemail box, no phone menu.

Frequently asked questions

What are the biggest local services ads updates in 2026?

Three stand out. Google is moving Local Services Ads into Google Ads as Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals (announced July 2026, phased from August 2026 into 2027). Since October 1, 2026, a missed call during business hours can be a charged lead if the caller stays on the line more than 20 seconds. And since November 17, 2026, historical reports can be downloaded from the redirection page.

When does my Local Services Ads account move into Google Ads?

Google's help page lists three phases: August 2026 for select US home and storefront service advertisers, late 2026 for broader groups such as service-area businesses and accounts with custom bidding or booking setups, and 2027 for non-US accounts and remaining categories. The account administrator gets an email 14 days before the migration date.

Does Google charge for missed Local Services Ads calls now?

It can. Google's How leads work page says a call you miss during business hours is a valid lead if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds, and a call that reaches voicemail during business hours is treated the same way. Calls outside business hours or your ad schedule that go unanswered are not charged. The change took effect October 1, 2026.

Can I still dispute Local Services Ads leads after the migration?

Google's migration help page says manual lead disputes are no longer supported in Google Ads. Google says it assesses lead quality automatically over 30 days and shows a Charge Status of Charged, Not charged, In review or Credited. You can still leave feedback through the survey on each lead. We make no promise about credits.

What happens to my LSA reports when the account migrates?

Google says past performance reports do not carry over to Google Ads, but past lead history does. Since November 17, 2026, you can download historical reports on the redirection page. The download covers lead totals (spend and charged leads split by phone, message and booking) and does not include impressions or clicks.
