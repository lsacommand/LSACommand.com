What Google Local Services Ads pay per lead means

With Local Services Ads, the business pays when a customer contacts it through the ad and Google counts that contact as a valid lead, not for each click.

Google's help page on the move into Google Ads states it plainly: "You still only pay for valid leads (such as phone calls and messages) rather than ad clicks." The FAQ on the same page adds bookings to that list (Google Ads Help, checked November 2026).

The pay per lead part has not changed. What changed in 2026 is which contacts count, where the campaign lives, and how budgets and bids work. For what leads usually cost, see how much Google Local Services Ads cost.

What counts as a charged lead under Google Local Services Ads pay per lead

Google's How leads work help page lists the contacts that count as valid leads. Google expanded that page in October 2026, as Search Engine Roundtable reported on October 8, 2026. Here is the current list, in plain terms:

Lead typeWhen Google counts it as a valid lead
Answered callYou answer, or you call the customer back and speak with them. Ring time does not matter for answered calls.
Missed callMissed during business hours, and the caller stays on the line more than 20 seconds.
VoicemailA voicemail or greeting during business hours counts as a connected call: charged after 20 seconds, message left or not.
MessageYou receive a text message or email from the customer.
BookingYou receive a booking request (United States and Canada only).

Google also says what is not charged. Leads it determines to be invalid or low quality are not charged, and its automated systems filter out invalid activity such as spam and duplicate contacts. Message leads are "typically priced lower than the corresponding phone lead price," but not always.

Each lead carries one of four charge statuses: Charged, Not charged, In review or Credited. Read that column weekly, not just the monthly total.

Missed calls and the 15-day rule in the Google Local Services Ads payment model

The biggest 2026 change is missed calls. Google emailed advertisers that "On October 01, 2026, we are updating how and when you are charged for call leads originating from Local Services Ads," and that missed calls during business hours "will now be charged as valid leads if a user stays on the line for more than 20 seconds" (Search Engine Roundtable, August 25, 2026).

The details from Google's help page:

  • Business hours are the hours in the Google Business Profile. If the ad schedule differs, missed calls are only charged during the active ad schedule and within business hours.
  • Phone menus. With a press-a-key menu, the 20-second timer resets when the caller presses the first key, and the call is charged if it continues more than 20 seconds after that. Later key presses in a multi-level menu do not reset the timer again. If the caller hangs up without pressing a key, there is no charge, even if the menu played longer than 20 seconds.
  • The 15-day rule. Once a lead is charged, follow-ups from the same phone number or email address are not charged again, provided there is interaction within 15 days. After 15 days with no activity, a new contact can be a new charged lead.

The Google Local Services Ads payment model after the move into Google Ads

Google is moving Local Services Ads into Google Ads as "a specialized Performance Max campaign type optimized specifically for pay-per-lead goals." The first phase started in August 2026 for select US home and storefront service advertisers, with more accounts in late 2026 and the rest in 2027 (Google Ads Help; Search Engine Roundtable, July 21, 2026). Ads still show only on Search and Maps, in the same positions.

The Google Local Services Ads payment model still charges per lead. What moves is how you control spend:

On the billing side, Google says Local Services Ads "use Google Ads to power its billing and payment systems," and accounts that already have Google Ads reuse those payment settings (How to pay for Local Services Ads). On Target CPA, Google warns that if the target is set too low to compete locally, ads may not serve (Lead quality and budgets). Lead quality, credits and disputes under the Google Local Services Ads payment model

Under the current Google Local Services Ads payment model, Google does the policing itself. Its help pages say leads are assessed at first contact, that "Charged leads get reassessed by our models over time," and that Google continues to assess lead quality over a 30-day period. Leads found to be poor quality, duplicate or invalid can be credited automatically (About automated lead credits).

Google says that "in most cases" credits are applied to the account balance within 30 days, and the original charge still appears on the invoice. It also "no longer supports credits for 'job type not serviced' and 'geo not serviced' leads," so service types and service area settings need to be right from day one. For campaigns with pay-per-lead goals, Google says "manual lead disputes are no longer supported." To flag a bad lead, you select it on the Leads page and fill in the Rate this lead survey, which starts an automated evaluation. Google also says call recordings and lead credits "aren't available for tax specialists or for health care verticals located within the United States."

Google decides every credit; nobody can promise one. What an agency controls is honest, prompt feedback on each lead. Our free LSA lead dispute helper helps you write that feedback, and how to dispute Google LSA leads covers the steps that still work.

Google Local Services Ads pay per lead terms: the agency checklist

Before a client runs pay-per-lead ads, they (or their agency) accept Google's Additional Terms for Ads with Pay-per-lead Goals (United States), last modified August 24, 2026. Google Ads is also rolling out a setting named "Ads with pay-per-lead goals terms" that advertisers must opt into to activate (Search Engine Roundtable, November 10, 2026). The terms are short. What we review with each client before anyone clicks accept (not legal advice):

Before the client accepts: 8 checks

  • Who accepts. The terms cover a customer "using the platform on an Advertiser's behalf," and say references to "you" also apply to Advertisers. Agree in writing who clicks accept and who is bound.
  • Provider requirements and verification. The provider must meet Google's Minimum Provider Requirements, give "honest, complete, and accurate information," and may be asked to recertify. Access can be denied if requirements are not met at any time. For accounts moving over, Google says "Insurance and license reverification are no longer required," and verified status and the Google Verified badge transfer to Google Ads automatically.
  • Call and message recording. The provider authorizes Google to "access, monitor, and record telephone calls, text messages, live chat" started through the program, must obtain the permissions local law requires, and must tell its team that calls may be recorded and get their consent.
  • Customer disputes. The terms say "ultimately it is up to you to resolve any disputes between you and your users." Google is not the referee on a job gone wrong.
  • Business hours and ad schedule. Missed calls are only charged during business hours and the ad schedule, so both must match when the phone is really covered.
  • Phone menu. If the client uses a press-a-key menu, the 20-second timer resets at the first key press, and later key presses do not reset it again.
  • Budget and bidding. Agree the daily budget and whether to use Maximize Conversions or Target CPA. Weigh volume against cost per lead before you pick.
  • Who rates leads. Name who fills in Rate this lead, and how fast.

Google can change these terms. Material changes take effect no sooner than seven days after posting, except changes for new functions or legal reasons, which can apply immediately.

Frequently asked questions

Is Google Local Services Ads pay per lead or pay per click?

Pay per lead. Google says you only pay for valid leads, such as phone calls, message leads or bookings, rather than ad clicks. That stays the same after Local Services Ads move into Google Ads as Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals.

What counts as a charged lead in Google Local Services Ads?

Per Google's How leads work help page: a call you answer or a callback where you speak with the customer, a missed call during business hours where the caller stays on the line more than 20 seconds, a text message or email, a voicemail, and a booking request (US and Canada only). Leads Google determines to be invalid or low quality are not charged.

Does Google charge for missed Local Services Ads calls?

Yes, in some cases. Since October 1, 2026, a missed call during business hours can be charged as a valid lead if the caller stays on the line more than 20 seconds. With a press-a-key phone menu, the 20-second timer resets at the first key press, and later key presses do not reset it. Missed calls outside business hours or outside the ad schedule are not charged.

Am I charged again when the same customer calls back?

Generally no. Google says follow-ups from the same phone number or email address are not charged again as long as there is interaction within 15 days. If 15 days pass with no activity, a new call or message can count as a new lead.

How does the Google Local Services Ads payment model work in Google Ads?

You still pay per valid lead. Weekly budgets become daily budgets (the weekly figure divided by 7), monthly spend is capped at the daily budget times 30.4, manual bidding is gone, and bidding uses Maximize Conversions or a single campaign-level Target CPA. In reports, Conversions are the leads you were charged for and Cost per conversion is your average cost per lead.

Can I still dispute a Local Services Ads lead?

Google says manual lead disputes are no longer supported for campaigns with pay-per-lead goals. Invalid leads are detected automatically, and filling in the Rate this lead survey starts an automated evaluation that can result in a change to the Charge Status. Google decides; nobody can promise a credit.
