Why LSA callers hang up: the five places LSA missed calls happen

A Local Services Ads caller has just tapped Call on an ad that sits next to other providers for the same job. They usually want to book now, and the next business is one tap away. None of the five places below is an ad problem. In every one, the caller reached the business's phone setup and gave up there.

1. On hold

A caller who lands in a hold queue can wait and give up. Hold music does not keep someone who has a list of other pros on screen. If a client's line ever queues LSA calls (a shared front desk, a busy dispatcher, a call center at peak times), this is the first thing to fix.

2. At voicemail

Someone who calls a plumber or a roofer from an ad usually wants a time slot, not a message box. When the phone rings out to a greeting, many callers hang up and try the next listing. Google's rules make this one expensive too, as covered in the next section: a call that reaches voicemail during business hours can still be charged.

3. During a transfer

Every transfer adds silence, ringing and a chance that the second line is busy or unattended. A call can drop between lines and leave no clear record of who should call back. LSA calls should land with someone who can book the job.

4. At a full voicemail box

This is the worst outcome for the caller. The call reaches voicemail and there is no room to leave a message, so the lead leaves no trace beyond the call log. It is also the easiest to prevent: check mailbox space on every client line.

5. In a phone menu

An interactive voice response menu ("press 1 for service") can lose a caller at the first key press. Menus also change how Google times a missed call, which matters for billing.

What Google counts as LSA missed calls

Google changed how it bills calls on October 1, 2026. Search Engine Roundtable (August 25, 2026) quoted Google's email to advertisers: "On October 01, 2026, we are updating how and when you are charged for call leads." Google's Local Services Help page How leads work (checked November 20, 2026) now lists this as a valid lead: "You missed a phone call during your business hours and the customer stays on the line for more than 20 seconds."

Four details from the same page map straight onto the five drop-off points above:

The same page says "The 20-second ring rule applies only to unanswered (missed) calls," and answered calls are evaluated on the conversation. Each lead shows its billing status in the Charge Status column on the Leads page in Google Ads: Charged, Not charged, In review or Credited. Google also says it assesses lead quality over time and may credit some leads, but nobody can promise a credit for any given call.

How to find LSA missed calls on each client account

Start with what the account shows, then test the phone line itself. Google is moving LSA management into Google Ads (see our guide to the LSA Performance Max migration for agencies), so where you open the leads may differ by client. The steps are the same.

  1. Pull the phone leads. Open the client's leads and look at call duration and the Charge Status column. Short calls and calls with no conversation are your first suspects.
  2. Listen to recordings where you have them. A recording tells you in seconds whether the caller met hold music, a greeting or a menu.
  3. Call the line yourself. Do it at a busy hour and again near closing time. Note whether you hit a queue, a transfer, a menu or voicemail, and whether the mailbox has room.
  4. Compare hours with real staffing. If the client's Google Business Profile hours and ad schedule say the business is open but nobody is on the phones, missed calls in that window fall inside business hours.
  5. Log it per client. A simple sheet with the six checks below is enough. Our free LSA missed-call cost calculator puts a dollar figure on what you find.

If you manage many accounts, the pattern usually repeats by client type, so one round of test calls tells you a lot. For what happens to a lead after nobody picks up, see what happens when a Local Service Ads lead calls and nobody answers.

Fix checklist for LSA missed calls

Free LSA missed call drop-off checklist (one per client)

  1. Hold queue: LSA calls never wait in a queue. Answer or roll to AI.
  2. Voicemail: no LSA call ends at a greeting during business hours.
  3. Mailbox space: if any line still uses voicemail, the mailbox is never full.
  4. Transfers: the first answer can book the job, with no hand-off.
  5. Phone menu: no press-1 menu in front of the LSA line.
  6. Hours: the Google Business Profile hours and ad schedule match the hours someone can really answer.

Local services ads missed calls: frequently asked questions

Why do LSA callers hang up before the business answers?

Most LSA missed calls happen in the phone setup, after the ad has done its job. Five common places: the caller waits on hold and gives up, hears a voicemail greeting and moves on, drops during a transfer, reaches a full voicemail box, or gets lost in a phone menu. A homeowner who taps Call on a Local Services Ad can call the next pro on the list.

Does Google charge for a missed LSA call?

It can. Google's How leads work help page says a phone call you miss during your business hours is a valid lead if the customer stays on the line for more than 20 seconds. If a phone menu requires a key press, the 20 second timer starts at the key press, and there is no charge if the caller never presses a key. Our post on LSA missed call charges covers the billing side in full.

Does Google charge when an LSA call goes to voicemail?

It can. Google says a call that goes to voicemail or an automated greeting during business hours is treated as a connected call and is charged if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds, whether or not they leave a message.

What does Google mean by business hours for LSA missed calls?

Business hours are the hours set in the Google Business Profile. If the ad schedule differs from those hours, Google only charges for unanswered calls that happen during the active ad schedule and within business hours.
