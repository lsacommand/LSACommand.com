What the Marchex data says about local services ads missed calls

Marchex, a call analytics company, published "Local Services Ads: What's Changing and How to Measure Calls That Drive Results" in November 2026. It covers "approximately 170,000 calls attributed to Local Services Ads since October 1st." The headline number: "6.2% of calls failed to connect the caller with the business." By our math that is about 10,500 calls.

Marchex then split those failed calls by where the caller dropped off:

Where the call diedMarchex wordingShare of failed callsShare of all LSA calls (our math)
On hold"abandoned while the caller was on hold"51.1%about 3.2%
Voicemail"abandoned after reaching voicemail"30.1%about 1.9%

Full mailbox"encountered a full voicemail box"6.3%about 0.4%
Phone menu"abandoned within an interactive voice response system"3.1%about 0.2%

Read the numbers with three caveats in mind:

  • It is one sample. The calls come from businesses on Marchex's platform, over roughly the first six weeks after Oct 1. Businesses that pay for call analytics may already watch their phones more closely than most, so we treat 6.2% as one data point, not a market average.
  • The five shares add to 100.1%. That is rounding, so the right column above is approximate.
  • It says nothing about billing. In Marchex's own words: "These classifications do not establish whether or not Google charged for individual leads." A caller who hung up was not necessarily a charged lead.

The pattern still matters. None of the five categories is an ad problem. In every one, the caller reached the business's phone setup (a queue, a greeting, a transfer, a mailbox or a menu) and gave up there.

Why LSA callers hang up: the five places LSA missed calls happen

1. On hold (51.1% of failed calls)

Half of the failed calls in the sample died in a hold queue. A Local Services Ads caller has just tapped Call on an ad that sits next to other providers for the same job, so the next business is one tap away. Hold music does not keep that caller. If a client's line ever queues LSA calls, this is the first thing to fix.

2. At voicemail (30.1%)

Nearly a third hung up after reaching voicemail. Someone who calls a plumber or a roofer from an ad usually wants to book now, not leave a message and wait. A recorded greeting ends the call for most of them, whether or not they leave anything behind.

3. During a transfer (9.5%)

About one in ten failed calls was lost while being passed from one person or line to another. Every transfer adds silence, ringing and a chance the second line is busy. LSA calls should land with someone who can book the job.

4. At a full voicemail box (6.3%)

This is the worst outcome for the caller. The call reaches voicemail and there is no room to leave anything, so the lead leaves no trace beyond the call itself. It is also the easiest to prevent: check mailbox space on every client line.

5. In a phone menu (3.1%)

A small share gave up inside an interactive voice response menu ("press 1 for service"). The share is small, but menus also change how Google times a missed call, which is covered next.

What Google counts as LSA missed calls

Google's Local Services Help page How leads work (checked November 20, 2026) lists this as a valid lead: "You missed a phone call during your business hours and the customer stays on the line for more than 20 seconds." Google started charging for some missed calls on October 1, 2026, as reported by Search Engine Roundtable (August 25, 2026), quoting Google's email to advertisers: "On October 01, 2026, we are updating how and when you are charged for call leads."

Three details from the same help page matter for the drop-off points above. With a phone menu, "the 20 second-timer will start once customers press the key," and you are not charged if the caller never presses a key. A call that reaches voicemail or an automated greeting during business hours is treated as a connected call, charged if the caller stays on the line for more than 20 seconds, whether or not they leave a message. Business hours are the hours set in the client's Google Business Profile, and unanswered calls only count when they fall inside both those hours and the active ad schedule (per the same How leads work page). Google also evaluates leads over time and may credit some, but nobody can promise a credit for any given call.

How to find LSA missed calls on each client account

Start with what the account shows, then test the phone line itself. Google is moving LSA management into Google Ads (see our guide to the LSA Performance Max migration for agencies), so where you open the leads may differ by client. The steps are the same.

  1. Pull the phone leads. Open the client's leads and look at call duration and charge status. Google's How leads work page says each lead shows its billing status in the Charge Status column on the Leads page in Google Ads: Charged, Not charged, In review or Credited. Short calls and calls with no conversation are your first suspects.
  2. Listen to recordings where you have them. A recording tells you in seconds whether the caller met hold music, a greeting or a menu.
  3. Call the line yourself. Do it at a busy hour and again near closing time. Note whether you hit a queue, a transfer, a menu or voicemail, and whether the mailbox has room.
  4. Compare hours with real staffing. If the client's Google Business Profile hours (and ad schedule) say the business is open but nobody is on the phones, missed calls in that window fall inside business hours.
  5. Log it weekly per client. A simple sheet with the six checks below is enough. Our free LSA missed-call cost calculator puts a dollar figure on what you find.

If you manage many accounts, the pattern usually repeats by client type, so one round of test calls tells you a lot. For what happens to a lead after nobody picks up, see what happens when a Local Service Ads lead calls and nobody answers.

Fix checklist for LSA missed calls

Free LSA missed call drop-off checklist (one per client)

  1. Hold queue: LSA calls never wait in a queue. Answer or roll to AI.
  2. Voicemail: no LSA call ends at a greeting during business hours.
  3. Mailbox space: if any line still uses voicemail, the mailbox is never full.
  4. Transfers: the first answer can book the job, with no hand-off.
  5. Phone menu: no press-1 menu in front of the LSA line.
  6. Hours: the Google Business Profile hours and ad schedule match the hours someone can really answer.

Run it on every client line once a month, and after any change to their phone system.

An agency can work through this list by hand on every account.
