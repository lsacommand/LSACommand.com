What the local service ads phone number on your ad actually is

The number a customer taps on a Local Services Ad is not the business's own line. Google's help page on managing leads and jobs (checked November 20, 2026) says:

  • "The number displayed on your ad is a Google forwarding number."
  • Calls are "forwarded to your actual business number."
  • It is "a dynamic number, meaning it will change often," and "There is no way to choose your tracking number."
  • When a call comes from the ad, the business hears "Call from Google," unless call forwarding or VoIP on the business line gets in the way.
  • In the US and Canada, inbound calls through the forwarding number are recorded by Google, and the customer hears a pre-recorded message first.

Google's general Google forwarding number page adds that "Since Google forwarding numbers associated with your call reporting are property of Google, they can change or be reassigned," and that "it's not recommended to copy or promote the Google forwarding number beyond your ad campaign with Google Ads." In plain terms: the local service ads phone number on the ad belongs to Google, it is there so Google can count and charge the lead, and nobody should save it as the customer's number.

That is how Google knows which calls came from the ad, and which ones to charge for. Our post on missed call charges covers how missed calls of more than 20 seconds now count too.

Why the local service ads number on a lead can carry an extension

The problem shows up later, when someone opens the lead and looks for a number to call back. Google's own lead data says the number you get is not always the customer's.

In version 25 of the Google Ads API, released July 22, 2026 (and in later versions), Google removed the consumer email field from Local Services leads and added a new field called phone_number_extension. The ContactDetails reference describes the two fields like this:

  • The phone number "can be a real phone number or a tracking number."
  • The extension "is populated only when the 'phone_number' is a tracking number." It is "typically 3 or 4 digits."
  • "When populated, this extension number is required to reach out to the right consumer."

So the local service ads number attached to a lead can be Google's tracking number, and the only way to reach the right person through it is to dial the number and then the extension. Our guide to the Local Services Ads API covers the other fields agencies can pull.

How LSA Command sends the real local service ads phone number

LSA Command works on the business side of the call, after Google forwards it. That is where the caller ID arrives. Here is the flow on every Google LSA call, the same one shown in the flowchart above:

  1. Google forwards the call. The customer taps the forwarding number on the ad, and Google sends the call to the business number on the account.
  2. Caller ID is captured. LSA Command grabs the caller's number from caller ID as the call comes in. No extension is attached.

Message leads: the local service ads number is a proxy on purpose

Message leads work differently, and Google is upfront about it. Its How leads work page says message leads are routed "through anonymized proxy numbers and email aliases," and the leads and jobs page says ongoing conversations run "through an anonymized alias." Google's Manage leads and jobs page also says a message lead's name, ZIP code, job details and phone number are shared with the provider, and to call the customer, you dial "the number listed on the lead page."

For message leads, speed is the lever. AI messaging replies to message leads instantly, so the customer gets an answer while they are still comparing providers. The notification then goes to the owner so the team can follow up.

Local service ads phone number checklist

  • The business phone number on the LSA account is the line that should ring, and it rings the team first.
  • Place a test call through the ad and listen for "Call from Google." If it never plays, check for call forwarding or VoIP on the business line, which Google says can block it.
  • The business hours in the client's Google Business Profile, and any active ad schedule on the LSA account, match when someone (or AI) can answer. Google's How leads work page says unanswered calls of more than 20 seconds are charged only during the active ad schedule and within business hours.
  • Nobody has copied the Google forwarding number onto a website, a truck or a CRM record. Google says these numbers can change or be reassigned.
  • The person who calls leads back knows that a number on a lead may be a tracking number, and that the extension is needed to reach the right customer.
  • The SMS notification number in the LSA account is a phone someone actually reads. Google says it does not have to be the business number.
  • Repeat callers are recognized. Google does not charge follow-ups again when there is interaction within 15 days from the same phone number, so knowing who is calling back matters.
  • Call recordings you may need for a dispute or a client question are downloaded promptly. Google's About Local Services data page says call recordings and messages are kept up to 180 days in North America (60 days for legal verticals).
