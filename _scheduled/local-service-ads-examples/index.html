Anatomy of local service ads examples in 2026

Google's Local Services Ads overview says the ad "highlights the most important information for customers to choose your business: services offered, service area, hours, and reviews." It also says the ad is "a preview of your business profile." Tap it and the full profile opens.

Part of the adWhat Google says it shows
1. Business name"The name of your business."
2. Rating and reviews"All ads will show an average star rating." The number of reviews "may be displayed in parentheses." Reviews come from the Google Business Profile.
3. Google Verified badgeShows "that your business is verified by Google." It replaced Google Guaranteed and Google Screened in October 2025 (more below).
4. Service area or city"Your business city may be displayed." In some cases Google shows "the cities you service instead."
5. Hours"The hours that your business is open."
6. Phone number"A tracking number will be displayed." Calls forward to the business phone.
7. Bio highlightsUp to 6 standard highlights, such as locally-owned and operated or 24/7 emergency service (Google).
8. Call, Message, BookCustomers can call, send a message or "schedule a booking with you directly through your ad," where available (Google).

Two caveats from Google. First, "the formatting of your ad may change from time to time" and alternate formats "may include more or less information," so you will see versions with fewer parts. Second, details like years in business are not on Google's list above, so we leave them out of the mockups rather than guess.

The badge changed in 2025. On August 20, 2025, Google announced that Google Verified would replace Google Guaranteed, Google Screened and License Verified by Google, and that the badge would "show alongside details of which verification checks a business has passed." Search Engine Roundtable (August 21, 2025) reported the switch took effect October 20, 2025. Any example you find with a green Google Guaranteed check is older than that. Our Google Verified badge guide covers what changed.

Where the ads show in 2026. Local Services Ads are moving into Google Ads as Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals, starting with select U.S. advertisers in August 2026. Google's transition page says the ads "will continue to show exclusively on Google Search and Google Maps in the same ad positions as before." So the examples look the same after the move.

Local services ads examples for home services: plumber, HVAC, roofer

Home service trades are where most people first see these ads. Here are three local services ads examples as a searcher might see them for "plumber near me," "hvac repair near me" and "roofer near me."

Example 1: Plumber. Anytown Plumbing Co. shows "Open 24/7," a 4.9 rating from 212 reviews and two highlights: free estimates and locally owned. The searcher here often has water on the floor. The ad's job is to get a tap on Call. The business's job is to answer it.

Example 2: HVAC. Anytown Heating and Air lists a wider service area ("Anytown and nearby") and an emergency service highlight. Heating and cooling calls are often urgent. If the phone rings out, the caller can simply tap the next ad in the unit.

Example 3: Roofer. Anytown Roofing Co. has fewer reviews (58). Roofing is a higher-ticket, slower decision, so the Message button can matter more: a homeowner may want to ask about an inspection before calling.

All three ads look alike on purpose. Google controls the format, so you cannot win on design. You win on the inputs Google uses (reviews, photos, verification, hours, service area) and on what happens after the tap.

Local services ads examples for lawyers and real estate agents

Professional services use the same unit with a few differences. Google says law professionals "have the option to add featured professionals and education details" to the profile, and Google's own product page shows a law office ad "which includes a button to schedule a visit." Real estate agent is on Google's list of categories eligible for the Google Verified badge on its Local Services Ads page.

Example 4: Lawyer. Anytown Family Law adds a Book button. Google says booking leads are available only in the U.S. and Canada and are not available for health care categories (Google). A booked consultation still turns into a call, so the firm needs someone, or something, ready to pick up. More in our guide to Google Local Services Ads for lawyers.

Example 5: Real estate agent. Anytown Home Realty has a perfect 5.0 from 41 reviews and two highlights. Agents are often out showing homes when the phone rings, which makes the missed-call problem below sharper for this category. See Local Services Ads for real estate agents.

What the best local service ads examples have in common

You cannot see Google's auction, but Google does publish what goes into it. Its About ad rankings page names three groups of factors: your bid; how likely your ad is to result in a lead, which covers responsiveness and profile quality ("Your rating, number of reviews, average response time, use of high-quality images, the verification checks you have completed"); and what the customer is searching for, which covers search context, relevance and whether message and booking leads are on.

How answering speed shapes the calls local service ads examples get

Every example above ends the same way: a person taps Call. From there, the ad has done its work and the business takes over. Two Google rules make that moment count more in 2026.

  • Missed calls can be charged. Google's How leads work page lists as a valid lead: "You missed a phone call during your business hours and the customer stays on the line for more than 20 seconds." Search Engine Roundtable (August 25, 2026) reported this took effect October 1, 2026. Details are in our post on LSA missed call charges.
  • Missed calls can hurt ranking. Responsiveness is part of how ads rank, and Google says missed calls may hurt it. Google's getting started page puts it plainly: "If you regularly fail to answer calls or respond to messages, your ad ranking may be affected."

Frequently asked questions about local service ads examples

What does a Local Services ad look like?

Google says a Local Services ad highlights services offered, service area, hours and reviews. It shows the business name, an average star rating with the number of reviews, the Google Verified badge, the business city or the cities it serves, business hours and a tracking number. Tapping the ad opens the provider profile. Google also says the format may change and can include more or less information.

Are the local service ads examples in this post real ads?

No. Every example is an illustrative mockup with a fictional business name, rating and details. The layout follows what Google's help pages say a Local Services ad and provider profile show, but no real business, customer or phone number appears.

Do Local Services Ads still show the Google Guaranteed badge?

No. Google announced on August 20, 2025 that a single Google Verified badge would replace Google Guaranteed, Google Screened and License Verified by Google. Search Engine Roundtable reported the change took effect October 20, 2025. Google says the badge appears with details of which verification checks a business passed.

Where do Local Services Ads appear?

Google says Local Services Ads show on Google Search and Google Maps. As accounts move into Google Ads as Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals, Google says ads continue to show on Search and Maps in the same ad positions as before.

Can customers message or book from a Local Services ad?

Yes, where it is available. Google says customers can call, send a message or schedule a booking through the ad. Messaging is available in select categories in the U.S. and Canada, and booking leads are available only in the U.S. and Canada and not for health care categories.

Does answering speed affect Local Services Ads?

Google lists responsiveness as a ranking factor and says missed calls may negatively affect it. Since October 1, 2026, Google can also charge a missed call during business hours when the caller stays on the line more than 20 seconds.
