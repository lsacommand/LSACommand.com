Where the Google LSA login is today

For an account that has not moved into Google Ads, the sign-in is the same as it has been. Google's help page on managing leads and jobs says you can "always sign in to your lead inbox on desktop" and links the inbox at ads.google.com/localservices/inbox. The menu in the top left holds Profile & budget, where message leads, pausing and notification preferences live. Google's getting started page still links an older inbox address on google.com, so if a bookmark looks different, that is why.

For an account that has moved, the same login takes you somewhere else. Google's page on the Local Services Ads transition to Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals says: "Attempting to log into your dashboard on or after your migration day will automatically redirect you to your campaign overview in Google Ads." The original dashboard is no longer available after that.

If you only need to know what the dashboard shows once you are in, our guide to the Google LSA dashboard covers that. This page is about getting in.

Which Google account to use for your local service ads login

There is no separate LSA username and password. Google's page on managing access to your Local Services Ads account says access is shared "through their Google account", and "the person you invite must have or create a Google Account". So the right login is whichever Google account was invited, or the one that created the account.

Two details cause most confusion:

  • The creating email gets the lead emails. Google's lead inbox help page says the email address used to create the account receives an email with a clickable link to find lead details in the lead inbox on desktop. If the owner set up the account with a personal Gmail, that is often the account that works.
  • A Google account with no access goes to sign-up. Google's Sign in to Google Ads page says that if you sign in to a Google account that is not associated with a Google Ads account, you are sent to the sign-up page, with the banner "It doesn't look like you have an account yet." That is a wrong-account problem, not a missing account.

To switch, Google says to click your profile image, choose Switch account, and sign in with the other email. The same menu lists the Google Ads accounts your current Google account can open, which is the quickest way to check whether you have access at all.

Google LSA login after the move into Google Ads

Google is moving Local Services Ads into Google Ads as a specialized Performance Max campaign type with pay-per-lead goals. Per Google's transition page, the first phase began in August 2026 for select US home and storefront services, broader groups follow in late 2026 (including service-area businesses without storefronts and accounts with custom bidding or booking setups), and non-US accounts and remaining categories follow in 2027. Search Engine Roundtable (July 21, 2026) covered the same timeline, and Search Engine Journal (July 20, 2026) reported the move.

Three things to know before migration day. First, Google emails the account administrator 14 days before, sends a final reminder 7 days later, and emails again when the move is done. If the administrator is a client's personal inbox, nobody at the agency may see it. Second, Google says to allow up to two weeks for the migration to finish. Third, on November 17, 2026, Search Engine Roundtable reported that historical reports can now be downloaded on the redirection page. Google says that download includes lead totals (spend and charged leads by phone, message and booking), not impressions or clicks.

Past leads, message history and older call recordings carry over, per Google. Verified status and the Google Verified badge carry over too. New Local Services Ads campaigns still start from Google's LSA sign-up at g.co/localservices for now. For the full agency runbook, see our LSA Performance Max migration playbook.

Local service ads login for agencies and manager accounts

Do not pass one client password around the office. Google's model is one Google account per person, each with its own access level. Per the access help page, an administrator goes to Admin > Access and security, clicks the plus button, picks a level, enters the email and selects Send invitation. The invited person accepts from the email. Pending invitations can be revoked from the same screen.

Google lists five levels: Email-only, Billing-only, Read-only, Standard and Administrative. Only Administrative can edit bidding and budget and manage users. Billing access is controlled separately in the Google payments profile.

For more than a handful of clients, a Google Ads manager account is the cleaner route. Google says Local Services Ads use Google Ads to set up manager accounts, they cannot be set up or changed from inside Local Services Ads, and linking is required for features like consolidated monthly billing. With one sign-in, your team switches between linked clients from the account list. Our LSA manager account guide walks through the link request and who should own what.

Local service ads login checklist for each client account

Run this once per client, ideally before the account migrates.

  • Write down which Google account created the LSA account, and who controls that inbox.
  • Confirm at least two people at the business have Administrative access, so one departure does not lock everyone out.
  • Give your agency access through a manager account rather than a shared password.
  • Set office staff to Standard and the bookkeeper to Billing-only.

Frequently asked questions

Where is the Google LSA login?

Sign in with the Google account that was invited to the Local Services Ads account, at ads.google.com/localservices/inbox. If the account has not moved into Google Ads yet, you land in the standalone lead inbox. If it has moved, Google says you are automatically redirected to the campaign overview in Google Ads, and leads are under Goals > Leads.

Is the local service ads login a separate username and password?

No. Google shares Local Services Ads access through each person's own Google account. The person invited must have or create a Google account, and that same Google account is the local service ads login.

Why does my LSA dashboard redirect to Google Ads?

The account has migrated. Google is moving Local Services Ads into Google Ads as a Performance Max campaign type with pay-per-lead goals, and says that logging in on or after migration day redirects to the campaign overview in Google Ads. The original dashboard is no longer available after that.

I signed in but I don't see the client's account. What should I do?

You are probably on the wrong Google account, or your access was removed or never accepted. Use your profile image to switch accounts. If it still is not listed, ask an administrator to invite your email from Admin > Access and security. If nobody with admin access is left, Google points to its Google Ads Account Access Request form, which asks for proof of ownership.

Can an agency sign in to many client LSA accounts with one login?

Yes, through a Google Ads manager account. Google says Local Services Ads use Google Ads to set up manager accounts, and they cannot be set up or changed inside Local Services Ads. Once a client accepts the link request, the account appears under your manager account.
