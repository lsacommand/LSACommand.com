Google local services ads credits appear as one of four charge statuses on every lead: Not charged, Charged, In review or Credited. Only Credited means Google issued a credit. A bonus credit for rating a lead gets its own decision: issued, over the cap, or not eligible. Count a credit only when the local services ads credit status says Credited.

Google local services ads credits start with four charge statuses

Every lead in a Google Local Services Ads (LSA) account carries a billing status. Google's How leads work page says you can track it "under the Charge Status column on the Leads page in Google Ads (marked as 'Charged,' 'Not charged,' 'In review,' or 'Credited')." Its page on automated lead credits defines each one.

StatusWhat Google saysCount it as a credit?
Not chargedLeads "that weren't charged from the onset," for reasons "including low quality or duplicate lead."No. It was never billed.
ChargedLeads "that were charged from the onset."No.
In review"This lead is being reviewed. If the lead isn't a good match for your business, a credit will be applied"Not yet. The review can end either way.
CreditedLeads where "you were charged and then credited. This may occur either through autocrediting or as a bonus credit for leaving feedback."Yes. This is the only one.

Google says "in most cases, credits will be applied to your account balance within 30 days." It also says "the original lead charge will still appear on your invoice." So the invoice and the lead list will not match. Our guide to Google Local Services Ads billing covers the invoice side.

Two kinds of Google local services ads credits: automated credits and bonus credits

Credited has two routes. Track them separately.

1. Automated credits. Google says leads "are first assessed when the potential customer makes initial contact, and leads determined to be invalid or low quality are not charged." After that, "charged leads get reassessed by our models over time, and may be issued credits automatically if later determined to be low quality." How leads work adds that Google "continuously assesses lead quality over a 30-day period." Search Engine Roundtable (June 25, 2024) reported Google's announcement that this automated process would replace manual credit requests starting in July 2024.

2. Bonus credits for feedback. Each lead has a "Rate this lead" survey, which Google calls the Lead Feedback Survey. Google says it "may also occasionally credit leads you report as poor quality through this survey." When a rating is sent through the Google Ads API, Google answers with a decision. The API reference lists four possible answers. Inside Google Ads, the Lead quality and budgets page describes the same loop: complete the Rate this lead survey on the Leads page, and the result shows under the Charge Status column.

Two are a yes: a bonus credit issued with the cap not reached, and one issued that brings the account to its cap. Two are a no: "Bonus credit is not issued because the provider has reached the bonus credit cap," and "Bonus credit is not issued because this lead is not eligible for bonus credit."

Two cautions. First, the reference gives no size for the cap, so we quote none. Second, timing: Google's response reference says "if a bonus credit is issued, it will be available for use in about two months." That is a different clock from the 30 days Google gives for automated credits. An approved bonus credit in one month's report may not be usable until about two reports later.

What Google local services ads credits no longer cover

Per Google:

  • Wrong job type and wrong area. "Google no longer supports credits for 'job type not serviced' and 'geo not serviced' leads." Fix the settings instead: see service area targeting and our post on bad Google LSA leads.
  • Some advertisers entirely. "Lead credits aren't available for health care verticals, tax specialists, or advertisers in EMEA."
  • Manual disputes. Google's transition page for the move into Google Ads states: "Manual lead disputes are no longer supported." What replaced them is covered in how to dispute Google LSA leads.
  • Missed calls. A missed call during business hours where the caller stays on the line more than 20 seconds is a valid lead under How leads work. See LSA missed call charges.

Why "In review" is not a credited local services ads credit status

The easiest mistake in an agency credit report is adding "In review" leads to the credited total. Google's wording even says "a credit will be applied" if the lead isn't a good match. But the sentence starts with "if." The review has not finished.

Google's API reference draws a similar line. A lead's credit state is either pending ("A credit has been filed and is being processed by Ads backend, but has not been applied to the account yet") or credited ("The credit has been issued to the Ads account").

The cost of the mistake is trust. If you tell a client five leads were credited and two later settle as Charged, next month's report has to take them back. Keep one line per local services ads credit status, and move a lead to the credited line only when Google does.

Monthly checklist: reconcile Google local services ads credits for each client

This free checklist works on any client account.

Monthly local services ads credit status checklist

  • Open the Leads page and read the Charge Status column for every lead in the month.
  • Count leads in four separate lines: Not charged, Charged, In review, Credited.
  • Report only the Credited line as credits. Show In review as pending.
  • Recheck last month's In review leads and record where each one landed.
  • Confirm every lead has a rating in Rate this lead, good and bad.
  • For each lead rated dissatisfied, record Google's answer: issued, cap reached or not eligible.
  • Flag approved bonus credits as "available in about two months," not as a credit the client can use this month.
  • Tell the client the original charge still appears on the invoice even when a lead is credited.

Frequently asked questions about Google local services ads credits

What are Google local services ads credits?

Google local services ads credits are credits Google applies to an advertiser's account balance for leads it decides were low quality or invalid. Google says charged leads are reassessed by its models over time and may be credited automatically, and that it may also occasionally credit leads reported as poor quality through the lead feedback survey.

How do I know if Google credited an LSA lead?

Check the lead's charge status. Google lists four: Not charged, Charged, In review and Credited. A lead is credited only when the status says Credited. Google says credits are applied to the account balance within 30 days in most cases, and the original charge still appears on the invoice.

What does In review mean on a Local Services Ads lead?

Google's definition is: "This lead is being reviewed. If the lead isn't a good match for your business, a credit will be applied." The review is not finished, so an In review lead is not a credit yet. Its local services ads credit status is still pending: report it that way and check it again next month.

What is a bonus credit in Google Local Services Ads?

It is a credit tied to the feedback you leave in Rate this lead. Google's API reference lists four decisions: issued with the cap not reached, issued with the cap now reached, not issued because the bonus credit cap is reached, and not issued because the lead is not eligible. An issued bonus credit is available for use in about two months.

Can I still dispute a Local Services Ads lead manually?

No. Google's transition page for the move into Google Ads says manual lead disputes are no longer supported. Credits are handled by Google's automated review, and the way to flag a lead is the Rate this lead survey.
